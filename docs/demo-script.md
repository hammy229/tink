# Demo Script

Target: about 2 minutes for the demo video. Beats marked (optional) can be cut to
hold that. Each beat says what to do on screen and what to say. The lines are a
guide, so say them in your own words.

The app, the model, and every number on screen are real. Only the microphone is
replaced: the non-word sounds are synthesized clips played into the app's mic
input, so nobody has to perform them. Say so in the video (beat 7).

## 0. Setup (before recording)

The demo runs from the `Tink-demo` folder next to this repo, which is kept out of
the repo so voice recordings can never be pushed.

1. **Bank the voice once (about 2 minutes).** Run `python3 record.py` in
   `Tink-demo`, open http://localhost:8771 in Chrome, and record the four
   phrases: "I'm thirsty.", "Please call my mom.", "I'm tired. Can we rest?",
   and "Help, it hurts!". Without them Tink uses the computer voice, and the
   "their own voice" beat doesn't land.
2. **Start the demo:** `./start-demo.sh` in `Tink-demo`. It starts the real
   server, teaches the six sounds if needed, banks the voices, and opens a
   Chrome window. Check the terminal says "4 banked voices ready."
3. **Keys** (press them with the Tink window focused, not inside a text box):

   | Key | Sound | Tink should |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | Low hum | say "I'm thirsty." |
   | 2 | In-between sound | ask "Do you mean ...?" |
   | 3 | Short mm (no) | take the answer no |
   | 4 | Short ah (yes) | take the answer yes |
   | 5 | Sharp cry (urgent) | raise the alarm |
   | 6 | Two clicks | say "Please call my mom." |

   Press **Listen** first, then the key. The terminal prints what Tink heard,
   so you can check each beat worked.
4. Speakers on and loud enough for the screen recording to pick up Tink's
   voice. Notifications off (macOS Focus mode). Do one run through every key
   before recording.
5. Record with QuickTime (File > New Screen Recording). Do a 10 second test and
   play it back to check both your mic and Tink's voice come through.

## 1. The problem (10s)

**Screen:** the Tink window, showing the list of taught sounds.

**Say:** "Tink is for people who can still make sounds but can't make words
anymore, like people losing their speech to ALS. Speech recognition doesn't help
here, since it's built to find words and throws everything else away."

## 2. Teaching a sound (15s)

**Screen:** open the low hum in the sound list so its takes and phrase show.
(Optional: scroll to **Teach a sound** to show the form.)

**Say:** "So first you teach Tink a sound. You record it about five times, type
what it means, and bank a recording of the person saying it while they still
can."

## 3. It speaks in their voice (15s)

**Screen:** press **Listen**, then **1**. Tink says "I'm thirsty." in the banked
voice.

**Say:** "Now when Tink hears that hum, it plays their own voice, not a computer
voice." Press **Listen**, then **6**: "Please call my mom." "Each sound gets its
own phrase."

## 4. It asks instead of guessing (20s)

**Screen:** press **Listen**, then **2**. Tink asks "Do you mean ...?". Answer
with **3** (no) or **4** (yes) to match what it asked, and let it settle on the
phrase.

**Say:** "If Tink isn't sure, it doesn't guess. It asks, and the person answers
with their yes or no sound."

## 5. Urgent sounds and answer-back (20s)

**Screen:** press **Listen**, then **5**. The alarm goes off. Let it run a
second, then click **I'm here**. Type "I'm coming, two minutes." in the reply
box and press **Say it**.

**Say:** "An urgent sound skips the questions, and the alarm keeps going until
someone answers. Then the caregiver can type a reply, and Tink says it out loud."

## 6. It runs in the browser (15s)

**Screen:** switch to the live site,
[tink-quen.vercel.app](https://tink-quen.vercel.app). Scroll from the top to
**Try it**, then to **Privacy** ("Their voice stays theirs.").

**Say:** "It runs on voc2vec, a model trained on non-verbal sounds. The live site
runs the whole model in your browser, so no audio is ever uploaded."

## 7. What it can and can't do (20s)

**Screen:** on the live site, the **Under the hood** section with the
**Measured** paragraph on screen.

**Say:** "On repeated words from 4 people with dysarthria, taught once and tried
on another day, Tink picks the right one of 5 about 62% of the time, against 20%
by chance. On spontaneous sounds from 8 minimally speaking people it gets 27%,
against 19%. It hasn't been tested with people using it to communicate, so this
is a prototype. The sounds in this demo are synthesized, and the voice is mine."

## 8. The proof (optional, 10s)

**Screen:** the GitHub repo ([github.com/hammy229/tink](https://github.com/hammy229/tink)),
scroll to the results and `docs/results.md`.

**Say:** "The method and every number are in the repo, generated by the eval
scripts."

## 9. Wrap-up (5s)

**Screen:** the Tink home page hero.

**Say:** "I'm Arham Doshi, and I started building this at HackTX. The link is in
the description."

## Things not to say

- Tink has not been tested with nonspeaking users. Don't imply it has, or that
  it's ready for clinical use.
- The 99% confidence on screen is for clean synthesized clips. Don't present it
  as real-world accuracy. The real numbers are the ones in beat 7.
- Say "I started building it at HackTX". Don't claim it won anything.
- No team details.
